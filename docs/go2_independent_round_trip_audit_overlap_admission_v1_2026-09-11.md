# Overlap admission for the monitored independent-study driver

The overlap verifier authenticates two exact completed development probes:
the four-arm factory CPU startup and the complete old 14-observation raw-audit
CPU replay. It checks their source/launch/output bindings, per-arm monitor
records, all 16 saved factory decisions against their reference, complete raw
audit equality and preserved failed navigation. Broader qualification flags
must remain false. Reactive execution does not require neural tensor use.

The population launch must bind the exact monitored worker/driver preparation,
monitor policy, one-collector/one-auditor staged bounds, probe identities and
the actual monitored driver entrypoint. The verifier and entrypoint are
top-level source-bound functions. The fixed entry wrapper passes this verifier
to the actual monitored driver, preserving the separately supplied final
population verifier. Monitor evidence is required for every actual future
independent audit and at its parent acceptance.

Quick checks rehash completed probe outputs and their reference decisions and
audit report. Initial and final full checks additionally call the completed
short probe's bounded-input admission, rechecking its original six-episode raw
bindings and corrected-model input bindings. They do not replay a model,
auditor, scene, training process or the full training ancestry. No new maze
sensor data are read. A boolean cannot substitute for this evidence reader.

This overlap component does not approve final policy, authenticate the new
population's full training/input ancestry or clear the existing native queue.
The original driver still requires all three through its separate final
verifier. No CLI or new native scene is provided here. Final policy review must
consider the queued navigation fixes before selecting a useful prospective
experiment. CPU instrumentation is not an OS device-access sandbox, and no
prior complete independent multiarm audit or collection/audit speedup is
claimed. The completed probes are bounded evidence; actual future audits
remain subject to runtime enforcement and failure rejection.
