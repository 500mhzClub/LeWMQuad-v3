This review reads the scientific aims from [the final SAINTS progression document](/home/andrewknowles/SAINTS_Year_1_Progression_Document_final-1.pdf), dated 29 August 2026, and checks them against repository source and development reports through 4 September. It excludes the superseded BEV programme as requested. It is a source-and-document review, not an independent reproduction of experimental results. No sealed material, external runtime artifacts, datasets, or checkpoints were opened, and no experiments were executed. Numerical results below are attributed to the preserved reports. The proposed experiments are a research plan, not amendments to existing frozen protocols or execution authorities.

The project has a credible scientific question and substantial useful infrastructure. Its strongest present evidence concerns predictive training and the diagnosis of failed interfaces. It has not yet demonstrated that JEPA rollout improves novel-maze navigation on a quadruped. The most promising next step is to establish a coherent sensor-to-action system, with an executable local controller and online memory, then measure the incremental value of prediction within that system.

The core question should be: **Does action-conditioned prediction of a compact, history-dependent sensor state improve exploration and goal reaching in unseen mazes, beyond a matched controller with the same sensors, representation, memory, and compute?** A second question asks whether predictive training improves the representation even when the predictor is omitted at deployment. These are different hypotheses and both are worth testing.

**What has been done well**

The progression document correctly separates representation, prediction, memory, hierarchy, and safety. Its recognition that a stationary robot can have few collisions while failing its task is particularly useful. Keeping gait control below the learned navigation stack also gives the research a tractable boundary. Its retrospective distinguishes a familiar-maze demonstration supported by heuristics from transferable navigation, instead of presenting the former as proof of the latter (PDF pp. 7–9, 31–34).

The counterfactual branching work is a strong experimental asset. The August assay evaluates 12 candidate actions from each of 20 reproduced states across eight families. This addresses a more meaningful question than ordinary next-frame prediction on a single behavior policy: whether a predictor distinguishes what happens under alternative actions. The report explicitly separates fidelity, branch retrieval, and occupancy evidence. It also discloses exact duplicate branch targets at short horizons. [Counterfactual evidence, lines 3–11 and 28–82](lewm_go2_counterfactual_predictor_qualification_v1_2_decision_ready_evidence_extract_2026-08-11.md).

The eight-seed proprioception-by-rollout study uses paired initialization, a prespecified estimand, fixed checkpoint selection, and uncertainty intervals. It reports a real improvement in its feature-prediction metric from rollout training, alongside a narrow null for the interaction with proprioception. It also discloses that its occupied-space co-outcome was unusable. That transparency is scientifically valuable. [Factorial report](lewm_go2_proprio_factorial_final_report_2026-08-10.md).

The more recent work asks whether even true future features support the proposed cost before trying to improve the predictor, and whether memory can help when the local controller cannot execute its instructions. These are good ways to identify the limiting component. Strong current-observation and kinematic baselines, candidate/time permutations, explicit oracle boundaries, and retention of failed results should all be preserved.

**First-principles assessment**

1. **The sufficient state is a belief derived from history.** Two corridors can produce identical current images while requiring different turns because one route was previously explored. An encoder of the current image cannot recover information that is absent from it. Short visual history helps infer motion, but persistent memory is needed for events outside that window. Hidden geometry should remain uncertain until observations distinguish it. Predicting a plausible corridor beyond a corner does not establish that this particular maze contains it.

2. **Predictability does not identify useful state.** A representation can encode floor texture, camera motion, or action frequency and achieve good prediction loss. Noncollapse only prevents a trivial representation; it does not require retaining thin obstacles, doorway width, goal identity, or stopping feasibility. Conversely, a representation need not reconstruct all geometry to support useful action choices. Qualify it on task-relevant distinctions and observable physical consequences. Spatial features are a sensible inductive bias, but a particular map decoder is not a scientific prerequisite.

3. **Feature distance is not travel cost.** Similar images may be far apart in the maze; adjacent views can differ substantially after turning. The failed true-future latent goal cost is therefore unsurprising. Use learned reachability, progress, and risk readouts, calibrated against executed outcomes, or an independently qualified local goal policy. Keep representation prediction and task cost distinct. A failed fitted readout on true futures is a diagnostic about that representation/readout/data combination, not a mathematical proof that every possible readout must fail.

4. **Long horizons do not automatically require learned dynamics.** In a static maze with predictable motion, geometry plus graph search may solve the task. Persistent memory can extend the horizon without a long autoregressive rollout. JEPA earns its place if it improves data efficiency, execution under uncertain dynamics, recovery, observation-aware decisions, or planning cost. Choose naturally relevant cases where those benefits are plausible, and also report their prevalence in ordinary generated mazes. A task constructed to require history establishes a capability challenge, not a population advantage.

5. **The robot is part of the transition model.** A command is not a displacement. Previous command, acceleration limits, gait, body attitude, slip, contact, actuator response, and sensing delay influence the outcome. Training on future measured motion would leak information unavailable during planning. The appropriate input is past sensing and the candidate command sequence; displacement and other realized consequences are targets. Useful proprioceptive state should be propagated through imagined futures, with uncertainty, rather than silently disappearing.

6. **A graph edge is an executable behavior contract.** “Go to the next node” leaves heading, approach side, clearance, arrival velocity, and edge completion unspecified. A successful local action must leave the robot in a state from which the next action remains possible. This is the link between immediate control and long-horizon navigation. The recent handoff work is pointed at the right problem.

7. **Observability limits and conservative certification limits differ.** The range contracts mark a transition risky if any protected-link/time witness lacks support. The source explicitly calls these conservative sufficient queries and says they do not prove hidden geometry cannot be inferred. Failure of this full-body direct-support gate is not proof that LiDAR, multisensor navigation, or uncertainty-aware local control cannot work. Temporal integration, robot shape, negative free-space evidence, restricted motion envelopes, and active sensing must be evaluated under their own justified assumptions. [Range contract, lines 1079–1104](../lewm/safety/body_centric_range_coverage_qualification_v1_contract.py).

**Specific things missed or underdeveloped**

The programme has stronger custody checks than semantic checks at some important boundaries. The renderer/schema mismatch, the median-defined occupied metric, batch-sensitive duplicate-image encoding, and the local execution failure all concern whether an experiment measures what its name suggests. The next development effort should prioritize small end-to-end semantic fixtures and observable behavior over additional architecture variants or new general auditing machinery. Preserve the existing custody rules; improve what is checked within them.

The proprioception null is narrower than it may appear. The corpus did not support useful contact, torque, or linear-acceleration channels, and excluded privileged body velocity. The implementation linearly projects five proprioceptive samples and averages them; this destroys within-slot temporal ordering, while applied-command history retains ordering. During rollout, observed proprioceptive slots decrease from three to zero. Thus the study does not test a strong persistent physical-state estimator. Its result should not discourage using properly measured IMU, joints, contacts, or estimated odometry. [Report limits, lines 246–252](lewm_go2_proprio_factorial_final_report_2026-08-10.md); [fusion and rollout source, lines 121–176 and 198–280](../scripts/dev_proprio_predictor_v1.py).

The latest good local ranker is not yet a physics-validated multisensor world model. Its challenge environment integrates commanded planar velocity and renders simplified wall views. The current physical handoff adapter passes visual tokens, relative target position/bearing, and command history into a frozen ranker. It does not pass sensed proprioception or depth. The older predictor-substitution source repeats one frame three times and supplies zero command history, a distribution mismatch that would need resolving in a future experiment; that substitution did not run in the August 31 result. [Motion/render source](../scripts/run_non_greedy_local_subgoal_jepa_planning_v1.py); [physical ranker, lines 4184–4212](../scripts/run_physical_graph_edge_handoff_qualification_v1.py).

The place-belief evidence needs simpler representation controls. The corrected test has 157 unique pixel arrays reused across many occurrences, and uses aligned-token cosine against template descriptors. A raw/downsampled-pixel descriptor, a simple visual descriptor, and frozen DINO features with the identical filter would establish whether the benefit comes from JEPA features or remembered observation sequences. Since MAP matches full belief and action shuffling has no effect, this test does not support an action-conditioned multi-hypothesis contribution. [Belief result](lewm_go2_occluded_goal_topological_belief_v2_result_2026-09-01.md); [observation and transition source, lines 2354–2398](../scripts/run_occluded_goal_topological_belief_v1.py).

Online graph construction remains a separate unsolved interface. The existing memory source documents a failure in which committing a new node reset the posterior to certainty and subsequently trapped localization. Its frozen-map workaround may help returning through a completed graph, but first-visit exploration must create and revise nodes while localization is uncertain. Delay irreversible merges, retain competing associations, and require corroboration before accepting loop closures. [Online memory, lines 75–104](../lewm/memory/online_topological_memory.py).

Qualification on teacher-successful states is legitimate for an initial feasibility experiment. However, resampling until every stratum contains qualified states changes the evaluated population to a teacher-conditioned subset. Report candidate acceptance and failure rates, including the difficult turning-junction and offset-opening strata, alongside model performance. Later end-to-end evaluation needs independently sampled start states and must count controller failures in task outcomes. A known-map search proves geometric reachability only; it does not prove reachability under the actual sensors, command bank, body dynamics, and stopping behavior.

**Recommended system**

Use RGB plus deployable proprioception and relative motion estimation as the principal research input. Treat RGB-only as an ablation. Compare an additional range/depth input separately if it is available on the actual platform; the repository's assumed L2 scans are development models, not a verified hardware configuration. Keep a clearly attributed independent local safety layer while assessing scientific navigation performance. This broadens the document's RGB-only premise in the direction explicitly requested here.

| Component | Proposed responsibility | Evidence it must supply |
|---|---|---|
| Spatial visual encoder | Retain local obstacles, openings, landmarks, and viewpoint information | Response to geometry changes; transfer across appearance; comparisons with frozen pretrained features |
| Causal multisensor state | Fuse short RGB history, IMU, joints, available contacts, command history, and relative odometry with validity/time information | Distinguish commanded from executed motion; remain useful under slip, delay, and missing sensors |
| Action-conditioned JEPA | Predict future spatial features and a compact physical state under candidate commands | Improvement over persistence/kinematics and correct counterfactual choices from matched histories |
| Task/risk readouts | Estimate progress to a local target, traversability, arrival state, and uncertainty | Low executed-action regret and calibrated errors, including on predicted states |
| Episodic place memory | Store multi-view keyframes, traversal evidence, visited branches, discovered beacons, and uncertain associations | Online construction, loop closure, revisitation, and recovery without an oracle graph |
| Global planner | Select a discovered goal or an unexplored edge | Exploration before first beacon sighting; avoid revisiting exhausted branches |
| Local controller | Execute a target defined by relative pose/region, heading, and completion criteria | Reliable edge crossing and a viable arrival state under actual Go2 dynamics |

Start with frozen spatial pretrained features and a modest temporal predictor to establish a useful reference. Then compare joint JEPA adaptation against the same reference. The PDF's distinction between a frozen representation and its intended jointly trained system is valid as a choice of experiment, but a frozen encoder does not make an action-conditioned joint-embedding model scientifically illegitimate. Frozen-feature approaches are established baselines. [DINO-WM](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.04983v1).

A practical JEPA objective predicts future spatial embeddings over a small number of action blocks, with a justified anti-collapse mechanism if the encoder is trainable. Add limited physical targets such as relative displacement, heading change, and execution reliability when they answer the task hypothesis. Keep simulator-only quantities on the training/evaluation side. Measure branch-specific effective updates, not just scalar loss magnitudes. Compare outcomes with and without predictive training to show whether auxiliary supervision alone explains the result.

Maintain separate local-dynamics and place-retrieval projections: local prediction benefits from viewpoint-sensitive spatial information, while retrieval must tolerate changes in viewing angle and illumination. They can share a backbone without being forced into one embedding or metric. Preserve pose/view hypotheses within a place rather than averaging every observation into one vector.

Begin with a small command/skill library that the actual frozen locomotion controller can execute. Consider short-horizon MPC only after its cost has been shown to rank actions. Use the graph for longer routes. A subgoal should describe a physically reachable corridor entrance, local target region, or remembered observation with approach constraints; it should not require an oracle image of an unseen destination.

The promising JEPA-specific avenue is predicting **execution-conditioned reachability and informative observations**: whether a turn will complete, whether an approach leaves room for the next turn, whether commanded motion will be lost to slip, and whether looking around a corner will resolve a place hypothesis. These are proposals to test, not capabilities inferred from the present results.

**A staged plan**

The following is an indicative 12–16 week development sequence for a core result, conditional on a working simulator/controller and available compute. Hardware transfer and statistical replication can extend it. It is a planning estimate, not a promise of completion or permission to resume frozen attempts.

| Stage | Work | Exit decision |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Weeks 1–2: scientific contract and semantic validity | Define sensor inputs, unknown-map task, goal information, runtime privileges, and motion envelope. Build a source-based evidence-lineage table. Specify and then run authorized fixtures checking rendered objects, camera pose, action timing, episode boundaries, meaningful sensor variation, and nondegenerate metrics. | A small complete evaluation can distinguish deliberately correct and incorrect systems, and its observations correspond to the intended physical scene. |
| 2. Weeks 2–4: local execution | Complete a prospective physical handoff experiment. Compare teacher/oracle geometry, practical geometric or range baseline, and current visual local policy on the same controller and action set. Test straight passage, both turn directions, offset doorway, dead-end exit, stopping, and consecutive edges. | Local commands reliably execute their stated target/arrival contract over a declared envelope. If oracle information also fails, address the controller/interface before changing the encoder. |
| 3. Weeks 3–6: useful training data | Collect authorized real-physics RGB/proprioception histories with short counterfactual branches. Balance forward motion, turns, stopping, recovery, and motion mismatch. Add a bounded development collection round from failures of the deployed local policy. | Branches contain meaningfully distinct outcomes, valid synchronized measurements, and enough transition coverage for the intended planner. |
| 4. Weeks 5–8: prediction that changes decisions | Train a frozen-feature predictor and compare joint adaptation. Test true-current, true-future, predicted-future, persistence, kinematic, and direct action-value readouts with matched information and training budgets. | Prediction improves a predeclared decision endpoint, or predictive training improves the no-rollout policy. If neither helps, retain the negative result and the strong baseline. |
| 5. Weeks 7–11: online memory and exploration | Start with an empty graph. Store observed traversals, frontiers, and beacon sightings; add conservative loop closure and revisitation. Compare simple descriptors, pretrained features, and JEPA-derived descriptors using the same memory algorithm. | Better coverage and remembered-goal return on unseen layouts, without ground-truth graph or hidden goal locations. |
| 6. Weeks 10–16: controlled integration | Run the rollout-by-memory factorial, sensor ablations, robustness tests, and paired maze-level analysis. Freeze a complete system for the designated untouched evaluation only after development decisions end. | A bounded positive claim or an interpretable negative result about JEPA, memory, and their interaction. |

Stage 1 should use visual intervention pairs: same pose/texture with one obstacle inserted or removed; same geometry with new texture; same scene with a known camera rotation. Compare visible changes with labels. Check contacts, torques, accelerations, and estimated velocities for actual variation rather than merely present fields. There is no need to inspect sealed material for any of these development checks.

Stage 2 needs both successful arrival and composability. A route ending at a waypoint while facing the wrong way or carrying excessive velocity may be a failure. Freeze the target's region, approach direction, heading tolerance, arrival speed, timeout, and recovery behavior before comparing rankers. Choose numerical tolerances from the physical platform and task, not to rescue a failed model. Record why teacher candidates were rejected. The September 4 stratified successor can establish the conditional panel it was designed for; its result must remain separate from an unconditional navigation claim.

Stage 3 should branch different commands from the same full simulator state while giving the learned model only deployment-valid history. Also test the same command in different scenes and under different physical conditions. All descendants of a scene and all branches from a parent state belong in the same data role. Randomize appearance independently of topology, route, action, and beacon placement. Include recoverable mistakes and bottlenecks, not only expert centerline trajectories. Keep training and test observation histories identical in form; do not evaluate a history-trained predictor using a duplicated current frame unless missing-history robustness is itself the experiment.

Stage 4 should measure executed progress and decision regret before expanding horizon or model size. Probe task costs on true future features first, but retain a strong direct current-history-plus-action predictor as a comparator: it can estimate outcomes without an explicit latent rollout. Restrict candidate search to the support demonstrated by data, because optimization can exploit even small model errors. Measure complete sensor-to-command latency and deadline misses. Optimize token count and planner candidates only after the comparison has a useful signal.

Stage 5 needs two different tasks: discovery with no known beacon location, and return to a beacon or place observed earlier. A supplied goal image and a supplied graph change the problem and should be separate conditions. Test aliases after long detours, false loop closures, reverse approach, kidnapped/reset localization as an explicit diagnostic, dead ends, and recovery from a wrong hypothesis. Add an unknown/new-place hypothesis so belief is not forced onto an existing node. Compare MAP with multi-hypothesis filtering only where observations and transitions actually leave ambiguity; do not assume full belief will outperform.

**Experiments needed for attribution**

The primary navigation comparison should cross memory and online rollout while holding sensors, representation initialization, action proposals, low-level control, safety layer, task budget, and planner compute as equal as practicable:

| | No persistent graph | Online persistent graph |
|---|---|---|
| Strong history-conditioned local policy, no rollout | Local baseline | Tests graph contribution |
| JEPA local rollout | Tests local predictive contribution | Tests combination and interaction |

Separately compare predictive pretraining with matched nonpredictive representation training, and frozen pretrained features with joint JEPA adaptation. Otherwise a positive graph-plus-JEPA result cannot distinguish graph benefit, better visual pretraining, and test-time prediction. A no-rollout baseline should retain comparable recent history; making it memoryless gives prediction an unnecessary information advantage.

Add RGB plus command history; RGB plus sensed proprioception/relative odometry; and, where relevant, RGB plus proprioception plus range. Pair each with a sensor-matched baseline. A range-only geometric system can be an engineering reference, but it is not a fair attribution baseline for an RGB-only claim. Test whether RGB actually changes decisions using natural geometry/appearance interventions, not only black images that are far outside the training distribution. Include no-RGB and shuffled-history ablations as secondary diagnostics.

Use a simple geometric navigation system and a competent visual goal-conditioned policy as external method families. NoMaD is a particularly relevant conceptual baseline: local visual behavior plus topological memory already supports exploration. Adapting its actual model is optional if integration costs are high; a clearly described, properly trained baseline with the same functions is more informative than a weak imitation of a published system. [NoMaD](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.07896v1).

Primary outcomes should be maze completion/beacons discovered within a fixed time or distance budget, with collision/fall and intervention measures specified alongside them. Also report coverage before the first sighting, time to discovery, return success, wrong-edge entries, repeated revisits, clearance violations, stopping failures, and latency. Declare how path efficiency is defined when the destination was initially unknown; a clairvoyant shortest path is an oracle reference, not an achievable expectation for first-visit search.

New random seeds of the same generator are only one kind of generalization. Separately evaluate new layouts, new motif combinations, larger/deeper mazes, appearance shifts, sensor changes, and motion/friction/latency shifts. Do not pool them into one unexplained “OOD” score. Include ordinary randomly generated mazes as well as targeted challenge sets.

Analyze paired differences on the same mazes. Episodes are clustered within mazes and maze families; branches and frames are not independent samples. Use an analysis that accounts for both environment variability and training seeds, such as a preregistered hierarchical bootstrap or an appropriate mixed model. Determine final scene/seed counts from development variability and a meaningful task-effect threshold. Eight training seeds on 20 fixed scenes do not substitute for environmental replication. Report all failed runs and candidate-generation exclusions in their correct category.

**Scope and literature corrections**

The final progression document is out of sync with the repository beyond the explicitly obsolete BEV material. Its plan to open sealed V4 (pp. 11, 13, 34) conflicts with the repository custody instruction that legacy V4 is permanently development-only and ineligible for final evaluation. That statement should be corrected; no legacy sealed role should be opened. Its current-position narrative should acknowledge the later predictive, planning, memory, and handoff results with their separate validity limits. Its claim that the raw dataset needs no rebuilding should become contingent on resolving the renderer and sensor-channel provenance.

The paper framing should distinguish a compact model target from the currently used large pretrained feature pipelines. Compactness is an empirical result measured by model memory, token count, end-to-end latency, and planning budget. It is not guaranteed by predicting latents. V-JEPA 2 demonstrates valuable action-conditioned robot planning, but its cited real-robot setup uses short-horizon manipulation and slow action selection, not autonomous maze exploration. [V-JEPA 2](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.09985v1).

LeWorldModel establishes stable compact joint training, but its simple navigation setting and its own discussion of short horizons/data coverage do not establish the present maze objective. Its reported mismatch between useful physical probes and planning is directly relevant here. Likewise, DINO-WM supports testing spatial pretrained features rather than assuming end-to-end training is necessary. The thesis contribution must come from controlled evidence about navigation and sensor-dependent execution, not from the presence of a JEPA module. [LeWorldModel](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.19312v2); [DINO-WM](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.04983v1).

A useful design reference is the systematic JEPA planning study, which evaluates multistep training, temporal context, spatial feature choice, and proprioception together. It reinforces the need to align data, state, and planner rather than treating objective choice as sufficient. [What Drives Success in Physical Planning with Joint-Embedding Predictive World Models?](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.24497v1). Legged navigation work also motivates using body feedback to change navigation decisions when motion fails, rather than merely appending sensor numbers to an image representation. [Coupling Vision and Proprioception for Navigation of Legged Robots](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2022/papers/Fu_Coupling_Vision_and_Proprioception_for_Navigation_of_Legged_Robots_CVPR_2022_paper.pdf).

Move limited real-sensor characterization earlier in the year, when separately authorized: camera timing/extrinsics, measured odometry, valid proprioceptive channels, and the command-to-motion response can reveal simulator assumptions before the complete learned navigator exists. This is different from deploying unqualified learned navigation. Keep the physical pilot within a defined low-speed environment and report interventions as part of system performance.

Defer a learned strategic H-JEPA until a flat local model plus graph works. The graph already supplies a temporal abstraction. A later strategic predictor should forecast edge executability, traversal time, arrival state, and failure probability, and be compared with ordinary graph search under matched memory and compute. Predicted latent subgoals require a separate executability test.

The strongest near-term paper would establish when predictive training and online rollout contribute to multisensor navigation, and when explicit memory or direct local control accounts for the gains. A result that prediction improves features but not planning is useful if the baselines and task support that interpretation. The immediate priority is a validated physical local-control interface connected to a valid observation stream; that is what allows the next JEPA experiment to answer the scientific question.
